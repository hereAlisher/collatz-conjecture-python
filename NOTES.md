## 📖 What the Research Says

 The Collatz Conjecture was proposed in 1937 by German mathematician **Lothar Collatz**,
who was 26 years old at the time. Despite nearly 90 years of attempts, no one has proven
it works for every positive integer — and no counterexample has been found either.

Computationally, it has been verified for all numbers up to **2⁶⁸** 
(roughly 295 quintillion). That's an enormous range, yet it still doesn't 
count as a mathematical proof — you can't check infinity by counting.

What makes it so hard to prove is that the sequence behaves unpredictably.
There is no known formula to tell you in advance how many steps a number 
will take, or how high it will peak. Mathematician Paul Erdős said about it:
*"Mathematics is not yet ready for such problems."*? -->
